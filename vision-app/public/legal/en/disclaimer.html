Medical Disclaimer

Version 2026-09-28 · Last updated 2026-09-29

We want you to know what SeeTuned can and cannot do. Please read this before your first check.

SeeTuned is not a medical device and does not replace an eye examination. If you have a sudden change in vision, eye pain, new flashes of light or floaters, or straight lines look bent – seek urgent medical care immediately.

1. What SeeTuned does

SeeTuned is a display-personalisation and viewing-comfort tool. It uses short on-screen visual checks to choose text size, contrast, colour and sharpness settings that are comfortable for you, and guides you to your device's accessibility settings. It also includes a magnifier and an enhanced photo and video viewer.

2. What SeeTuned is not

  • It is not a medical device.
  • It does not diagnose, screen for, monitor or treat any disease or condition.
  • It does not provide a prescription for glasses or contact lenses.
  • It does not replace an eye examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist.
  • It is not for use in emergencies.

That is why results are shown as display settings (for example "Recommended text size: 150%" or "Colour filter"), not as medical measurements or a glasses prescription. A "good" result in SeeTuned does not mean your eyes are healthy.

3. Why a screen cannot correct blur

Glasses and contact lenses correct blur by bending light before it enters the eye, so that the image is focused on the retina. A normal screen only emits light: it cannot focus that light for your eye, so it cannot optically correct blur caused by the way your eye focuses.

What a screen can do is make reading easier: larger text, stronger contrast, colour filters and crisper edges. These adjustments can make viewing more comfortable, but they do not replace glasses or lenses. If you use glasses, wear them while using SeeTuned.

4. Results depend on conditions

Results are an estimate for viewing comfort only, and they are affected by:

  • the size, quality, brightness and settings of your screen;
  • room lighting and reflections on the screen;
  • the distance between your eyes and the screen;
  • whether you were wearing glasses or lenses;
  • tiredness, concentration and how you answered.

Results may therefore vary from one time to the next and between devices, and you should not draw conclusions from them about the health of your eyes.

5. When to see an optometrist or ophthalmologist

We recommend booking an examination with an optometrist or ophthalmologist if:

  • you have not had your eyes examined for a long time, or your recommended routine check is due;
  • reading or seeing is still hard after tuning your screen;
  • you notice a clear difference between your right and left eye;
  • your vision is changing gradually, or you have headaches, eye strain or discomfort;
  • the app recommends that you have your eyes checked;
  • you have any question about your eyes or vision.

6. When to seek urgent care

Go to an emergency department, an urgent-care centre or an eye doctor the same day if you have:

  • a sudden loss of or change in vision, even in one eye only and even if it has passed;
  • eye pain, or a red and painful eye;
  • new flashes of light or floaters, especially if they appear suddenly;
  • a shadow or "curtain" over part of your vision;
  • distortion – straight lines that look wavy or bent, or a spot in the centre of your vision;
  • sudden double vision;
  • an injury to the eye, or a chemical in the eye.

In an emergency, call your local emergency number (in Israel: 101, Magen David Adom). Do not rely on SeeTuned in these situations, and do not wait to "check again" in the app.

7. Safe use

  • Do not use the magnifier or the app while driving, cycling or walking.
  • If you feel dizzy, get a headache or feel discomfort during a check, stop and rest.
  • During long periods of screen use, take short breaks and look into the distance.
